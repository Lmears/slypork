Practical guide

Find the space between the hits

The kick’s tail, the bass and the duck share the same moment. Shape them together, with both voices playing.

  1. Get a loop going.

    Hold a note on Init, as in your first pattern. Ducking is already on.

  2. Decide where the kick ends.

    On the kick’s Amp tab, shorten the curve’s tail or its Length. A shorter kick leaves the bass more room.

  3. Draw how the bass comes back.

    Open the bass’s Duck tab. The curve is the bass’s volume after each kick.

    Move the rise earlier for a tight bass, later for more space. Bend a line to change how it returns, or pull the end down for a pump.

    The Duck knob sets the depth; lower it to keep some bass under the kick.

  4. Check the first bass hit.

    A bass hit that starts with the kick can end before the duck lets it through. Lengthen the bass or move the rise earlier. Later hits in a held note land after the duck has recovered.

Or swap the bass for rumble

Mute the bass. On the kick’s More tab, turn up Reverb Amt with Size up and LP down. The tail follows the same duck curve.
