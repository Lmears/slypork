Practical guide · Ableton Live

Duck the rest of your mix to the kick

KerBy sends a MIDI note every time the kick hits. Route it to a ducking plugin on a group, and pads, drums or a whole bus pump with the kick.

  1. Load the VST3.

    In Live, only the VST3 version of KerBy sends MIDI out; the AU can’t. Put it on its own MIDI track and get a pattern playing.

  2. Make a MIDI track that listens to KerBy.

    Add an empty MIDI track. In its MIDI From, choose KerBy’s track in the top menu, then KerBy in the menu below it, the third option under Pre FX and Post FX.

    Set the track’s Monitor to In, so the notes pass through without arming it.

  3. Put a MIDI-triggered ducker on the group.

    On the group you want to duck, add any ducking plugin that a MIDI note can trigger, such as ShaperBox, LFOTool or Devious Machines’ Duck. Switch its trigger to MIDI, then draw a volume shape that dips at the start and rises back.

  4. Send the notes to it.

    Back on the new MIDI track, set MIDI To to the group, and the menu below it to the ducking plugin on that group.

    Press play. The group now ducks on every kick, and follows KerBy as it moves: change the kick’s Rate or turn up Glitch, and the duck goes with it.

Mute the kick, lose the duck

A muted kick sends no notes, so the group stops ducking too. To keep the pump without hearing the kick, turn the kick down on its track instead.
