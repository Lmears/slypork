Practical guide

Your first kick & bass pattern

Hold one note and KerBy plays both voices.

  1. Hold a note in Live.

    Load KerBy on a MIDI track, start from Init and choose Live. Hold a note, or draw one long note in a clip.

    Init’s kick Rate is 1/4 and the bass’s is 1/16: a kick on every beat, with the bass filling the gaps.

  2. Turn Rate, then come home.

    Near the end of a phrase, turn the kick’s Rate faster for a stutter. Press the home button to bring both rates back, on the beat.

    Map Return Home to a controller button in your DAW.

  3. Set how long each hit lasts.

    Start the bass’s Length short, then raise it until the gaps fill.

  4. Keep the kick on one note.

    To play a bassline under a steady kick, set Key on the kick’s More tab. Leave the bass’s on MIDI and play legato.

  5. Leave a few gaps.

    Open the step row from the bottom bar. Tap a box for a gap, drag one up a few semitones, or drag a step’s right edge to make a longer note.

  6. Or write it in Draw.

    Switch to Draw and write a rhythm in a clip. Each note plays one kick and one bass hit, so make it long enough for the bass to come back after the kick.

If the bass seems to vanish

Usually it’s under the duck. A bass hit that starts with the kick can end before the duck lets it through: hold the note longer, lengthen the bass, or bring the duck up sooner.
