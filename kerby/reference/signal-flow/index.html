Signal flow

Follow the sound.

From the two sources to the space between them.

Checked against KerBy 0.7.0 · 8 September 2026

The kick and bass are generated separately. The kick also feeds a reverb, and its pulses restart the ducking curve that controls both the bass and the reverb tail.

Select a box to read more. On a narrow screen, scroll the map sideways.

Inside the kick

The main source and the Click layer have separate envelopes. They join before Crush and the kick’s volume control.

Main source

  1. Oscillator or Wave samplePitch, FM and Spread act here
  2. + Noise
  3. Tone
  4. Amp envelope

Click layer

  1. Click sampleOwn Pitch and optional key tracking; FM also reaches it
  2. Tone
  3. Click envelope
  4. HP → Click Amt
  1. Combine main + Click
  2. Crush → VolumeThen split into dry kick and reverb send

Tone uses the same setting on both branches, before each branch’s envelope. Crush reduces the sample rate of their combined output. Noise and Spread shape the main source, leaving the Click layer’s own sound separate.

Inside the bass

  1. Oscillator or Wave samplePitch, FM and Spread
  2. Filter + ResoCutoff follows the Filter envelope
  3. + NoiseNoise is shaped separately to follow the cutoff
  4. Tone → Amp envelope
  5. Crush → Volume
  6. Duck gainThen Reverse and meters / scopes

The noise joins after the main filter, but has its own filtering that follows the same cutoff. Turning off the Filter tab opens the cutoff; it does not remove the stages that follow it.

Where the voices meet

Reverb
The kick’s output feeds the send. Reverb and its LP filter come before ducking. The ducked tail rejoins the dry kick, with level compensation as the send increases.
Duck
Kick pulses restart the curve. The resulting gain, including Duck Amt and bypass, multiplies the bass and the wet reverb. It does not multiply the dry kick.
Reverse
Works on each voice’s finished audio, after ducking and reverb. A reversed kick therefore includes its reverb tail.
Meters and scopes
Read the finished voices after Reverse, before the master sum and output clipper.
Output
The two voices are added together, then pass through an always-on soft clipper. A voice’s meter can show a peak above 0 dB even though the final output is bounded.

This map describes the sound path. The pulse clock and launch settings decide when a voice starts; the bass step row decides which pulses play. They are controls on the path, rather than effects inserted into it.
